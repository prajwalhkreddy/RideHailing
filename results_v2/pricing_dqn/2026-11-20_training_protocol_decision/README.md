# Proposed hierarchical OD training and evaluation protocol

**Status: decision packet for approval; not a launch authorization.** No training, inference, simulation, protected-data access, model edits or runtime changes were performed. This packet uses the completed [16-slot development experiment](../2026-11-20_full_episode_development/README.md), targeted source inspection and master §§5.3, 10.4–12. The proposed configuration is descriptive and is not compatible with the existing runner without the bounded implementation work below.

Use the cutoff-safe January historical-average demand forecast, January OD support and January–February sensitivity. This is the **historical-average-demand hierarchical research arm**, not a CNN-driven result, independent generalization study or privacy-enabled experiment. Architecture, action support, rewards, masked routing loss, half-squared Bellman loss, equal-client aggregation and optimizer settings remain unchanged.

The exact window is **March 1 00:00 EST to March 12 17:00 EDT**, end exclusive: 280 elapsed hours, 560 slots. `episode_plan.csv` specifies every UTC and New York boundary. This single pass covers mornings, afternoons and evenings without rearranging demand. The March 8 clock change produces no nonexistent local slots. March 12 17:00–24:00 (14 slots), and development-date hours outside the specified evaluation windows, are explicitly outside this experiment; they are not discarded from the dataset. No full-March request-conservation claim is made. Within the selected windows, every source row must reconcile to an eligible request or an unchanged documented exclusion; every eligible request appears once, with supported/fallback accounting.

Start pricing and routing from fresh seed-42 initialization, **not** the March12 pilot checkpoint. Otherwise earlier training states would receive later learned information. Generalize the existing March12-only provenance contract honestly: fitting sources remain January/February, runtime experiences become eligible only when realized within this newly approved chronology. Existing hyperparameters and design choices have already been exposed to March development evidence; this is not independent model selection.

At each eight-hour boundary, retain pricing online/target weights, Adam state, replay, update count and RNG streams; retain routing global/local weights, local Adam states, private memories and absolute federation round. Reset physical fleet, energy, queues, commitments and operational priors to the same documented initialization, using seed 42, preserving driver identities and EV membership. ## Exploration and learning mathematics

`ODPricingDQN.epsilon` currently computes `max(0.05, 1 − 0.95*u/50000)`, where `u` counts successful **pricing optimizer updates**. It does not decay per action or slot. Warm-up below 1,000 replay entries prevents an update and hence prevents decay. One completed slot supplies 9,572 transitions, so all 16 pilot slots were warm and received eight updates each. The observed 128 updates reproduce 0.997568 exactly.

Current thresholds: epsilon ≤0.5 requires 26,316 updates (206 completed episodes); ≤0.2 requires 42,106 (329); ≤0.1 requires 47,369 (371). The 0.05 floor is reached at 50,000 updates (391 episodes). These are arithmetic projections assuming 128 updates/episode, not a recommendation to train that long.

Master §5.3 and §10.4 describe a 0.1 floor and permit 0.005 or 0.01 decrements every ten iterations. Recommend **`epsilon(u) = max(0.1, 1 − 0.005*floor(u/10))`**, explicitly defining an iteration as one successful optimizer update. This needs approval and a new version; it has not been installed. It reaches 0.5 at 1,000 updates, 0.2 at 1,600 and 0.1 at 1,800 (within episodes 8, 13 and 15). End-of-episode epsilon is 0.94 at episode 1, 0.555 at 7, 0.105 at 14, and 0.1 from 15 onward. This uses the smaller master-described decrement and leaves roughly 20 episodes at the floor, without adding data passes or selecting epsilon from observed revenue. `exploration_trajectory.csv` includes the full comparison.

Keep Adam 0.001, gamma 0.99, batch 64, replay 100,000, warm-up 1,000, eight updates per warm slot and soft tau 0.005. The target is the maximum of the target network, not epsilon-greedy; loss is half the mean squared Bellman residual. These are inherited calibrated settings, not all manuscript-mandated numbers. The master's warm-up example is guidance; retaining the tested configurable 1,000 avoids a second unmotivated change.

## Development and protected evaluation

Evaluate frozen copies at episodes **7, 14, 21, 28, 35** on these fixed development windows: March13 00–08, March14 08–16, March15 16–24 New York time. Use epsilon=0 and disable both pricing and local routing optimizer updates. Preserve private driver utility decisions and charging safety; frozen routing weights still provide origin-specific response probabilities. Evaluation must not modify training replay, private memories, optimizers, RNG or model-selection provenance. Subsequent chronological training remains uncontaminated by evaluation experiences; development scores are explicitly permitted selection information.

Select maximum mean successful-assignment **system revenue** across the three windows, breaking ties in favor of the earlier checkpoint. Report supported learning reward and fallback revenue separately. Require all correctness gates. At/after episode21, if the last three scheduled scores fail to improve the preceding best by at least 1% of `max(abs(best), 1 USD)`, stop for development plateau; report failure to establish further improvement, not convergence. Training revenue alone and decreasing training loss are insufficient. No parameter changes after viewing these scores.

Once selected, evaluate three fixed-price comparators on the same windows: lowest frozen configured action, reference Pbase (explicit non-DQN benchmark), highest frozen configured action. Use the **selected checkpoint's same frozen routing estimator** for all policies to isolate pricing. These comparators run once after selection, not with different routing checkpoints during selection. March16 is a development-only confirmation: three disjoint eight-hour windows and the selected policy plus the same three comparators, with no further checkpoint selection or tuning. Independently reset fleets per window; this is not continuous-day charging evidence.

All policy comparisons require identical eligible request IDs, exclusions, fallback rules, seed-42 fleet/EV identities, initial SOC, initial priors, weather protocol and request-keyed sensitivity draws. Branch-dependent RNG consumption must not silently destroy pairing; validate alignment before execution. Record assignment-time revenue, acceptance/rejection, assigned/accepted-unmatched, completion and terminal outstanding counts, repositioning counts/distance/cost, charging navigation/queue/start/completion/wait/energy, fleet invariants, and supported/fallback breakdowns. One seed supplies reproducibility, not statistical uncertainty across runs.

The current Softplus CNN was selected using March1–15 labels, so it cannot be backdated into March1–15 operational states. No CNN arm is proposed for this training pass. ## Replay diversity and routing scalability

The runtime creates one transition per January-supported OD context per slot: 9,572 contexts ×16 =153,152 per episode. FIFO eviction preserves the latest 100,000, equivalent to ten full slots plus 4,280 contexts from the preceding slot (5.5 hours touched). A sorted insertion boundary can partially retain the older slot; this is documented, not changed. All 9,572 OD identities remain represented. Next states use the following UTC boundary after operational updates and permitted FedAvg feedback. Terminal records still carry next-state arrays, but their bootstrap is zero.

Read-only inspection of the saved final replay found **98,281/100,000 zero-reward transitions**. For the full episode, at least 137,094/153,152 contexts had no supported request and at least 149,033 had no successful dispatch, using conservative distinct-context upper bounds from request/assignment counts; these are bounds, not exact generated counts. The update/transition ratio is 128/153,152 =0.00083577; 8,192 sampled presentations per episode are only 5.35% of generated transitions before accounting for repeated samples. Uniform replay samples OD-time contexts rather than requests. This is a defensible faithful implementation of the current objective but has sparse feedback and rapid eviction; it does **not** establish sample efficiency or learning adequacy. Preserve all semantics pending evidence, with no pruning, priority sampling or extra updates.

The pilot had 4,288–4,958 participating clients/slot, mean 4,774.1875; 76,387 genuine updates, 629,508 sample presentations and 16 rounds. Source inspection confirms **one update per active client on its latest at most 32 observations**, not a full epoch over 1,000 observations. Local Adam state persists; local weights start from the latest global weights. Only current-slot participants upload, equally weighted, and results affect subsequent slot decisions. Private memories remain private; NONPRIVATE refers to aggregation, not a privacy guarantee.

For 35 episodes: 4,480 pricing updates, 5,360,320 generated transitions, 560 rounds and approximately 2,673,545 local updates at pilot participation; absolute maximum 2.8 million updates and 89.6 million sample presentations. Increased batch fill can slow kernels; private memory/checkpoint growth also matters even though update count stays bounded. Do not subsample clients or change weighting to meet the budget.

Measured primary wall time was 37.32 minutes, slot computation 28.68 minutes, and local optimizer time 930.29 seconds (54.07% of slot time). Linear training projection is **21.77 hours**. Up to 36 evaluation windows give 71 total eight-hour runs: a same-cost proxy of **44.16 hours**. Evaluation has no gradient updates but daytime requests, checkpointing and longer histories can cost more. Reserve up to **96 wall-clock hours**, with a safe stop rather than automatic extension. These are planning estimates from one overnight window, not performance guarantees.

## EV validation, storage and stopping

Zero observed charging events do not establish charging performance. Mean EV energy after the first slot was 40.31 kWh and at the end 38.38 kWh; only eight hours elapsed. The configured initialization draws bounded SOC and charging uses an energy threshold. Aggregate energy alone cannot prove that every vehicle stayed above its trigger; per-vehicle threshold-crossing evidence is absent from the compact report. Do not change SOC to manufacture activity.

Before EV-performance claims, require a separately authorized **continuous 24-hour, no-reset, no-learning** March16 development validation with existing SOC initialization, safety rules and event-level energy/queue accounting. Start with 10% EVs; if no charging occurs, report insufficient charging exposure, not successful charging-performance validation. Final experiments must retain 10%, 20%, 30% scenarios, each with frozen matched initialization/protocol. This task does not authorize any of those runs or automatic horizon extension.

Retain at most **three full resumable checkpoints** for the new run: latest recovery, best development checkpoint, latest seven-episode milestone; replace obsolete generations only after atomic write, checksum/manifest validation and pointer commit. Save recovery every four slots and at episode completion, never permanently per slot. At most one additional staging generation may coexist. On failure restore the last verified boundary with all models, private memories, optimizers, replay, fleet, clocks and RNG; no silent fresh restart. Preserve all old evidence/checkpoints outside this new retention scope.

Pilot peak RSS was 4.58 GB and two checkpoints totaled 2.84 GB. These are lower-horizon observations; do not assume each future checkpoint stays 1.42 GB. Proposed guards: stop safely at 16 GiB RSS, checkpoint >20 GiB, new-run usage >100 GiB, or free disk <50 GiB / insufficient space for an atomic replacement. Three 20-GiB retained generations plus one staging generation require up to80 GiB before logs. Actual memory/serialization growth needs first-boundary validation. Stop on finite-loss/gradient failure, reward/request mismatch, invalid probabilities, fleet/EV failure, clock leakage, replay identity corruption or RNG/resume mismatch; save only valid state. At a resource limit pause for a decision, never weaken participation or conservation.
