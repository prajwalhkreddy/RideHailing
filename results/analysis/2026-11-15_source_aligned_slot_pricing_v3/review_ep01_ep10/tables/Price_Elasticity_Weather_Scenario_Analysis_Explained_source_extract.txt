Step 2: Calculate the mean and standard 
deviation
Mean elasticity 
is the average elasticity in a 
scenario:
Mean elasticity = (sum of all elasticity observations) / 
n
Here, n is the number of valid elasticity observations. The mean summarizes the average price 
sensitivity.
Standard
 
deviation
 
describes
 
how
 
widely
 
the
 
elasticity
 
values
 
are
 
spread
 
around
 
their
 
mean.
 
A
 
smaller
 
value indicates values are more closely grouped; a larger value indicates greater spread.
Step 3: Cluster similar 
scenarios
Clustering means grouping scenarios whose elasticity statistics are similar. For example, clear and cloudy weather might be candidates for one group if their mean elasticity and spread are close. Rainy weather might remain
 
separate
 
if
 
its
 
elasticity
 
differs.
 
The
 
grouping
 
must
 
be
 
supported
 
by
 
a
 
defined
 
similarity
 
measure,
 
not
 
just by the weather labels.

Recommended implementation 
procedure
Clean the complete 
dataset
Check missing or invalid values and ensure fare, demand, weather, and time information are 
usable.
Define 
scenarios
Separate observations by the selected weather classes and/or time 
slots.
Calculate 
elasticity
Apply the approved price-elasticity formula separately to each 
scenario.
Calculate 
statistics
Record observation count, mean elasticity, and standard deviation for every 
scenario.
Compare 
scenarios
Use a defined similarity index or clustering method to identify candidate 
groups.
Test 
combinations
Calculate combined elasticity statistics without merging raw records for joint model 
training.
Report 
findings
Present individual results,
 accepted and excluded groups, and the implications for customer 
sensitivity.
